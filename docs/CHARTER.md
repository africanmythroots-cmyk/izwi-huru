# The Izwi Huru Pan-African Charter

**Version 1.0 — Draft for External Review**
*Last revised: September 2026*

> *"Izwi" means voice in Zulu. "Huru" means free in Swahili.*
> *This platform exists to ensure that freedom of speech is not merely a legal
> concept, but a lived reality — that what is spoken here cannot be used
> against the person who spoke it.*

---

## Preamble

Across Africa, freedom of expression is formally protected in the laws of most
nations. Yet people are prosecuted for social-media posts, detained for
criticising government officials, and threatened for speaking inconvenient
truths. The law protects the speech; the state sometimes punishes the speaker.

Izwi Huru exists to close that gap. It is a pan-African discussion platform —
not tied to any single country, political party, government, ethnic group, or
ideology. It is governed by this Charter, which is public, permanent, and
cannot be overridden by any moderator, funder, government request, or internal
decision without a public amendment process.

This Charter distinguishes between two kinds of speech:

1. **Speech that must be protected** — criticism of power, dissent,
   investigative journalism, satire, minority voices, uncomfortable truths.
2. **Speech that harms people** — doxxing, incitement to violence, targeted
   harassment, ethnic hate speech, threats.

The line is drawn here. It is drawn clearly and in public so that no moderator
can move it arbitrarily, and no government can weaponise our moderation
against our users.

---

## Part I — Scope and Applicability

### 1.1 Who This Charter Governs

This Charter applies to:
- All content posted, replied to, amplified, or shared on Izwi Huru
- All users, regardless of country, language, or verified identity
- All moderators at every tier, including the Escalation Panel
- The Izwi Huru organisation itself, including its staff and administrators

### 1.2 Territorial Neutrality

Izwi Huru does not recognise any single government's definition of
"acceptable speech" as binding. A post that is legal in one African country
but illegal in another is evaluated against **this Charter only**, not against
the laws of the country where the user is located or where the content is
most discussed.

No government, individually or collectively, can direct Izwi Huru to remove
content solely because that content is inconvenient to that government, absent
a showing of harm under the terms of this Charter.

## Part II — Protected Speech

The following categories of content are **actively protected** by this Charter.
Moderation actions that target this content for political reasons are a
violation of the Charter, not an enforcement of it.

### 2.1 Criticism of Government and Public Power

Criticism of elected officials, unelected power-holders, government policies,
government institutions, military commands, and public institutions — including
sharp, angry, or satirical criticism — is fully protected.

This includes:
- Allegations of corruption, with or without proof (opinion is protected; false
  statements of fact that are presented as fact are not — see §3.4)
- Calls for accountability, resignation, or protest
- Documentation of human-rights abuses
- Comparisons of leaders to historical figures, including unflattering ones

Criticism of power does not become harassment merely because the powerful
person dislikes it.

### 2.2 Investigative and Citizen Journalism

Reports and accounts documenting events, policies, and actions of public
concern are protected. This includes:
- First-hand witness accounts of violence, protest, or state action
- Document leaks published in the public interest
- Verification threads correcting official narratives
- Aggregate data and analysis of government records

### 2.3 Satire and Political Commentary

Satire, parody, and political comedy — including content that exaggerates,
mocks, or ridicules public figures — is protected, provided it is reasonably
recognisable as satire and does not cross into the categories of §3 (threats,
doxxing, incitement).

### 2.4 Minority and Dissenting Voices

Voices that challenge majority positions — religious, political, ethnic,
cultural, linguistic — are protected. The majority position on any question
does not become a standard below which all other speech is prohibited.

### 2.5 Personal Testimony

First-person accounts of discrimination, violence, surveillance, or harassment
by individuals in positions of power are protected, including accounts that
name the individual responsible, provided they describe documented or alleged
personal experience (not invented accusations).

## Part III — Prohibited Content

The following categories of content are **not permitted** on Izwi Huru. These
prohibitions are narrow, specific, and enforced on the basis of the actual harm
caused — not on whether a government, funder, or vocal group finds the content
offensive.

### 3.1 Doxxing

Publishing personally identifying information about a private individual
without their consent, where that information could foreseeably be used to
locate, surveil, or harm them. This includes:
- Home address, workplace, or daily routine of a private person
- Real name linked to a pseudonymous identity
- Family members of a public figure who have not themselves entered public life
- Information that could identify an anonymous source

**Note**: Publishing already-public information about a public figure (e.g. a
politician's official office address) is not doxxing under this Charter.

### 3.2 Incitement to Violence

Content that explicitly calls for physical violence against an identifiable
person or group, or that provides material assistance (instructions, resources,
coordination) for carrying out such violence.

Political anger, even intense anger, is not incitement. The test is whether a
reasonable person reading the content would understand it as a genuine call to
commit violence against specific people.

### 3.3 Targeted Harassment

Coordinated or persistent conduct directed at a specific individual with the
purpose of causing distress, fear, or silencing them. This includes:
- Organising others to flood a user's posts with abuse
- Sustained personal attacks that go beyond criticism of their expressed views
- Impersonating a user to damage their reputation

A single critical reply is not harassment. Criticism of someone's ideas is not
harassment. The pattern and purpose matter.

### 3.4 Ethnic and Racial Hate Speech

Content that dehumanises, threatens, or calls for discrimination against
people on the basis of ethnicity, race, national origin, religion, caste, or
similar group characteristic. This includes:
- Content portraying an ethnic group as subhuman or as an existential threat
  warranting elimination
- Calls for ethnic cleansing, genocide, or mass expulsion

Criticism of practices, institutions, or political formations associated with
a group is not hate speech under this Charter. Historical discussion,
including of atrocities, is not hate speech.

### 3.5 Threats

Credible, direct threats against the physical safety, liberty, or livelihood
of a specific identifiable person.

### 3.6 Non-Consensual Intimate Content

Sharing intimate images, video, or descriptions of a real person without their
consent.

### 3.7 Coordinated Inauthenticity

Operating multiple accounts or coordinating with others to artificially
amplify content, suppress others' content, or misrepresent the scale of
support for a position. Includes impersonation of journalists, moderators,
platform administrators, or verified community members.

## Part IV — Moderation Structure

### 4.1 Two-Tier System

Moderation on Izwi Huru operates in two tiers:

**Tier 1 — Local Moderators**
Moderators assigned to a specific Space (a language/country community within
the platform). Local moderators can:
- Place content in a review queue pending escalation
- Add content warnings to posts
- Lock threads that have devolved into sustained harassment

Local moderators **cannot**:
- Permanently remove content they classify as political speech
- Act unilaterally on any content that may be protected under Part II
- Remove content without citing the specific Charter clause it violates

**Tier 2 — Escalation Panel**
A standing panel with members drawn from at least three different African
countries. The panel:
- Reviews any action escalated from Tier 1 by users or moderators
- Has final authority on whether content violates the Charter
- Can permanently remove content, suspend accounts, or overturn Tier 1 decisions
- Publishes all significant decisions in the Transparency Log (see Part VI)

### 4.2 Moderator Selection and Term Limits

- Local moderators are nominated by the community of their Space and ratified
  by the Escalation Panel
- Panel members serve terms of 18 months, renewable once
- No single country may hold a majority on the Escalation Panel
- Moderators may not moderate content from their own country's government
  requests — a conflict-of-interest rule requiring automatic recusal

### 4.3 The Charter Is Not Negotiable by Moderators

No moderator, at any tier, may create a local rule that contradicts this
Charter. Local Spaces may have additional rules governing tone, topic focus,
or language — but they may not narrow the protections of Part II or widen the
prohibitions of Part III beyond what this Charter permits.

### 4.4 User Appeals

Any user whose content is removed or account actioned may appeal:
1. To the local moderator (if action was taken by Tier 1)
2. To the Escalation Panel (within 30 days of any Tier 1 decision)
3. The Panel's decision is final unless the Charter itself is amended

Appeals are decided within 14 days. Users receive a written explanation citing
the Charter clause for any adverse decision.

## Part V — Government Requests

### 5.1 Logging All Requests

Every request received from a government authority — takedown demands, user
data demands, identity disclosure demands — is logged immediately upon receipt,
regardless of whether Izwi Huru complies. This log is public (see Part VI).

### 5.2 Evaluation Against This Charter

Izwi Huru evaluates every government request against this Charter, not against
the requesting government's domestic law. A request to remove political
criticism will be refused even if the content is technically illegal in the
requesting country.

### 5.3 What We Will and Will Not Comply With

**We will act on** a government request that identifies content that also
violates this Charter (e.g., credible threats, doxxing). In these cases, we
would have acted regardless of the government request.

**We will refuse** requests to remove content that is protected under Part II
of this Charter, regardless of the legal framing of the request.

**We will not disclose** any user information. We do not retain IP addresses,
device identifiers, or any data that would allow us to identify a user behind
a pseudonym. There is no user data to compel.

### 5.4 User Notification

Where a government request results in action on a user's account or content,
the user will be notified of the action and the fact that a government request
was involved, unless a court order specifically prohibits notification. In that
case, the action will be logged in the Transparency Log as "notification
withheld" with a reason code.

## Part VI — Transparency

Izwi Huru publishes a Transparency Report every quarter. Each report includes:

- Count of government requests received, by country
- Count of requests complied with and refused, by reason category
- Count of moderation actions at Tier 1 and Tier 2
- Count of user appeals and their outcomes
- Any material changes to this Charter and the process that produced them
- Any incidents in which the platform's resilience was tested (domain blocking,
  infrastructure pressure, legal threats)

Reports are:
- Published in full on the primary website, all mirror domains, and the Tor
  hidden service address
- Published in Swahili and English (and additional languages as capacity allows)
- Signed with the platform's public PGP key
- Submitted to the Internet Archive

## Part VII — User Rights

### 7.1 Right to Pseudonymity

Every user has the right to participate under a persistent pseudonym. Izwi Huru
will never link a pseudonym to a real identity. Moderation decisions are made
on the content, not the person behind it.

### 7.2 Right to Explanation

Every moderation action carries a written explanation citing the Charter clause
violated. "We removed it because it violates our rules" without specifying
which rule and why is not an acceptable explanation under this Charter.

### 7.3 Right to Appeal

Described in §4.4.

### 7.4 Right to Leave

Any user may delete their account and all their content at any time. Deletion
is irreversible and complete. We retain nothing after deletion.

### 7.5 Right to Access the Charter

This Charter is always publicly accessible — on the main website, every mirror,
and the Tor hidden service. It cannot be paywalled, login-gated, or removed.

---

## Part VIII — Charter Amendment

This Charter may be amended only through the following process:

1. A proposed amendment is published publicly for a minimum 30-day comment period
2. Comments are accepted from all registered users and the general public
3. The Escalation Panel deliberates and votes (two-thirds majority required)
4. The amendment, including all public comments received, is published in the
   Transparency Report for that quarter
5. Amendments that narrow the protections of Part II require additionally:
   - A 60-day comment period
   - Review and non-objection from an independent digital-rights organisation

No emergency process exists for narrowing user protections. Speed does not
override the amendment process.

---

## Part IX — Funding and Editorial Independence

### 9.1 No Funder Controls Content

No grant, donor, partner, or funder of Izwi Huru gains any influence over
what content remains on the platform. Funding relationships are disclosed
publicly. Any funder that attempts to influence content decisions will have
that attempt disclosed in the Transparency Report, and the funding relationship
will be terminated.

### 9.2 No Advertising

Izwi Huru does not carry advertising. Advertisers cannot buy placement, boost
posts, or receive user data of any kind.

### 9.3 Funding Sources

Izwi Huru is supported by:
- Grants from digital-rights, press-freedom, and civil-society foundations
- Voluntary membership contributions from users and diaspora communities
- Anonymised aggregate insight reports sold to academic and research
  institutions (never individual-level data; never to governments)
- Institutional partnerships with journalism organisations and universities
